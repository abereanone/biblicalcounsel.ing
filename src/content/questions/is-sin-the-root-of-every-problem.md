No. Some troubles come from our own sin, some from others' sins against us, and some from living in a fallen world with weak bodies. Wise counsel tells these apart and responds to each as Scripture does.

## Proofs
- John 9:1-3
- 1 Thessalonians 5:14
- Job 1:1
- 2 Corinthians 12:7-10
- Psalm 34:18

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

When Jesus' disciples saw a man born blind, they assumed someone's sin had caused it. Jesus answered, "Neither this man nor his parents sinned" (John 9:3). Job was "blameless and upright" (Job 1:1), and he suffered terribly. Paul's thorn in the flesh was not his fault (2 Corinthians 12:7).

Paul gives counselors a helpful map: "admonish the unruly, encourage the fainthearted, help the weak, and be patient with everyone" (1 Thessalonians 5:14). Different people need different responses:

- **The unruly** need loving correction.
- **The fainthearted** need encouragement and hope.
- **The weak** need practical help.
- **Everyone** needs patience.

A counselor who treats a grieving widow as if she were rebellious will crush her. A counselor who treats a rebellious man only as a victim will leave him in his sin. Most people are some mixture: sinned against, sinning in response, and weak.

Even when suffering is not caused by sin, it is still the place where we are called to trust and obey. And sin is never the whole story, because God is "near to the brokenhearted" (Psalm 34:18).
