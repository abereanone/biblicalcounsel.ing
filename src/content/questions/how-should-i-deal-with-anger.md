Recognize that most sinful anger comes from desires that are being frustrated. Slow down, examine what you want, confess sinful anger to God and those you hurt, and put on patience, kindness and forgiveness.

## Proofs
- James 1:19-20
- James 4:1-2
- Ephesians 4:26-27
- Ephesians 4:31-32
- Proverbs 15:1

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Not all anger is sin. God is angry at evil, and "Be angry, yet do not sin" (Ephesians 4:26) assumes anger can be righteous. But most of our anger is not about God's honor. It is about our own desires: "What causes conflicts and quarrels among you? Don't they come from the passions at war within you? You crave what you do not have" (James 4:1-2).

Steps toward change:

1. **Slow down.** "Everyone should be quick to listen, slow to speak, and slow to anger, for man's anger does not bring about the righteousness that God desires" (James 1:19-20).
2. **Ask what you wanted.** Respect, control, comfort, convenience, to be right? Anger often reveals a desire that has become a demand.
3. **Repent specifically.** Confess the anger and the demand behind it to God, and go to those you have hurt.
4. **Deal with it quickly.** "Do not let the sun set upon your anger, and do not give the devil a foothold" (Ephesians 4:26-27).
5. **Put on the opposite.** "Get rid of all bitterness, rage and anger... Be kind and tenderhearted to one another, forgiving each other just as in Christ God forgave you" (Ephesians 4:31-32).
6. **Use soft words.** "A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger" (Proverbs 15:1).

If your anger has become threatening or violent toward anyone, get help immediately and make sure others are safe.
