God hates divorce and calls married couples to keep their vows. Scripture permits divorce in two situations: sexual immorality, and the desertion of a believer by an unbelieving spouse. Even then, reconciliation is the first hope, and every case should be walked through with the elders of your church.

## Proofs
- Malachi 2:16
- Matthew 19:3-9
- 1 Corinthians 7:10-15
- Ephesians 4:32

"'For I hate divorce,' says Yahweh, the God of Israel" (Malachi 2:16). Divorce tears apart what God joined together, and it wounds spouses and children deeply.

Jesus explained that Moses allowed divorce "because of your hardness of heart. But it was not this way from the beginning" (Matthew 19:8). He then gave one exception: "whoever divorces his wife, except for sexual immorality, and marries another woman commits adultery" (Matthew 19:9).

Paul adds a second situation. If an unbelieving spouse leaves a believer, "let him go. The believing brother or sister is not bound in such cases" (1 Corinthians 7:15). Many Reformed churches apply this also to a professing believer who abandons the marriage and refuses the church's correction.

Some guidance:

- **These are permissions, not commands.** Even after adultery, a couple may by God's grace rebuild their marriage, and many have.
- **Separation is not the same as divorce.** Paul allows that a spouse may separate, while calling for reconciliation (1 Corinthians 7:10-11). Separation for safety in cases of abuse is right.
- **Get help early.** Talk to your pastor long before you are talking to a lawyer.
- **There is grace for the divorced.** Divorce is not the unforgivable sin. Christ receives repentant sinners and heals the wounded.
