By knowing one another, praying for one another, listening carefully, sharing God's Word in everyday conversation, encouraging the fainthearted, gently restoring the straying, and bringing harder situations to the pastors.

## Proofs
- Romans 15:14
- Colossians 3:16
- Galatians 6:1-2
- 1 Thessalonians 5:14
- Hebrews 3:13

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

The New Testament's "one another" commands describe a church full of counselors: teach and admonish one another (Colossians 3:16), "exhort one another daily" (Hebrews 3:13), "carry one another's burdens" (Galatians 6:2), "consider how to spur one another on to love and good deeds" (Hebrews 10:24).

You do not need a degree to do this. You need love, the Word, and wisdom. Some practical habits:

- **Know people.** Ask how they are really doing, and remember what they told you.
- **Listen well** before you speak (James 1:19).
- **Pray with them**, right then, not only "I'll pray for you."
- **Share Scripture** that has helped you, simply and personally.
- **Fit the help to the need.** "Admonish the unruly, encourage the fainthearted, help the weak, and be patient with everyone" (1 Thessalonians 5:14).
- **Restore gently.** "You who are spiritual should restore him with a spirit of gentleness. But watch yourself, or you also may be tempted" (Galatians 6:1).
- **Know your limits.** When someone is in danger, when abuse or crime is involved, or when a situation is beyond you, involve your pastors.

The best counseling in most churches happens over meals and in hallways, not in offices.
