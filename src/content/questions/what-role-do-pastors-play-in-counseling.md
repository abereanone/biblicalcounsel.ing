Pastors are shepherds who must watch over the souls of their flock, so personal counsel from the Word is part of their calling. They counsel, train others to counsel, and oversee the care of the whole congregation.

## Proofs
- Acts 20:28
- 1 Peter 5:2-3
- Hebrews 13:17
- Colossians 1:28-29
- Acts 20:20

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Paul told the Ephesian elders, "Keep watch over yourselves and the entire flock of which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers. Be shepherds of the church of God" (Acts 20:28). He reminded them that he had taught "publicly and from house to house" (Acts 20:20).

A shepherd does not only feed the flock from the pulpit. He knows the sheep, seeks the wandering, binds up the injured and protects them from wolves. Counseling is the personal side of that work.

Pastors serve in several ways:

- **They counsel** members directly, especially in serious or complex situations.
- **They equip** the congregation to counsel one another (Ephesians 4:12), through teaching, training and example.
- **They oversee.** They know who is struggling and make sure no one falls through the cracks.
- **They protect.** They guard against harmful teaching and act to protect the vulnerable, including by involving civil authorities when crimes are reported.
- **They do it willingly and humbly**: "not out of greed, but out of eagerness; not lording it over those entrusted to you, but being examples to the flock" (1 Peter 5:2-3).

Pastors "watch over your souls as those who must give an account" (Hebrews 13:17). That is a heavy calling, and the church should pray for them and support them in it.
