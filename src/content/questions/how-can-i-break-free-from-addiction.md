Scripture describes addiction as being mastered by a desire, a form of worship that promises life and delivers slavery. Freedom comes through repentance and faith in Christ, who breaks sin's power, together with honest accountability, practical barriers and the help of the church, and medical care where the body needs it.

## Proofs
- 1 Corinthians 6:12
- Romans 6:12-14
- 1 Corinthians 10:13
- Titus 2:11-12
- John 8:34-36

Paul refused to "be mastered by anything" (1 Corinthians 6:12). Jesus said that "everyone who sins is a slave to sin" (John 8:34), and that "if the Son sets you free, you will be free indeed" (John 8:36).

Addiction is not only a habit or a disease, though it affects the body powerfully. At heart it is a form of worship: the substance or behavior is trusted to give relief, escape, comfort or pleasure that only God can truly give.

Steps toward freedom:

1. **Be honest.** Stop hiding and minimizing. "He who conceals his sins will not prosper, but whoever confesses and renounces them will find mercy" (Proverbs 28:13).
2. **Turn to Christ.** "Sin shall not be your master, because you are not under law, but under grace" (Romans 6:14).
3. **Get medical help if needed.** Withdrawal from alcohol and some drugs can be dangerous. Do not stop suddenly without a doctor's advice.
4. **Make no provision.** "Make no provision for the desires of the flesh" (Romans 13:14): remove the substance, block the websites, change the routes, end the relationships that feed it.
5. **Use God's way of escape.** "He will also provide an escape, so that you can stand up under it" (1 Corinthians 10:13). Plan in advance what you will do when temptation comes.
6. **Walk with others.** Regular, honest accountability with mature believers.
7. **Fill the empty place.** Ask what the addiction promised you, and seek that in Christ and in obedience.

Relapse is not the end. Confess, get up, and keep going.
