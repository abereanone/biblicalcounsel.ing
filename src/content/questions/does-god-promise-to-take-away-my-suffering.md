God promises to be with His people in suffering, to give them grace enough for it, and to end all suffering forever when Christ returns. He does not promise to remove every trial in this life, and sometimes His answer is grace to endure.

## Proofs
- 2 Corinthians 12:7-10
- Isaiah 43:2
- John 16:33
- Revelation 21:4

Paul begged the Lord three times to remove his "thorn in the flesh." God's answer was not removal but grace: "My grace is sufficient for you, for My power is perfected in weakness" (2 Corinthians 12:9). Paul learned to say, "when I am weak, then I am strong" (2 Corinthians 12:10).

Jesus was plain: "In the world you will have tribulation. But take courage; I have overcome the world!" (John 16:33).

What God does promise:

- **His presence.** "When you pass through the waters, I will be with you" (Isaiah 43:2). Notice: *through*, not *around*.
- **Sufficient grace** for each day's trouble.
- **Purpose** in every trial for those who love Him (Romans 8:28).
- **An end.** "He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and there will be no more death or mourning or crying or pain" (Revelation 21:4).

It is right to pray for healing and relief, and God often gives it. Teachers who promise that faith always brings health and wealth are not speaking for God, and their promises leave sufferers feeling that the fault is theirs.

> **From the *Catechism of Suffering*, question 51:** Will suffering last forever?
>
> "No. For those in Christ, every sorrow will end when death is destroyed and all things are made new."
>
> Matthew Statler, [suffering.catechize.ing](https://suffering.catechize.ing/questions/cos-51)
