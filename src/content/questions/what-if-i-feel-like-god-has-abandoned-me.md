Many faithful believers have felt that way, and Scripture gives them words to say it to God. But God's presence does not depend on our feelings: He has promised never to leave His children, and Christ was forsaken on the cross so that they never will be.

## Proofs
- Psalm 22:1
- Psalm 13:1
- Hebrews 13:5
- Isaiah 49:15
- Matthew 27:46

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

"My God, my God, why have You forsaken me?" (Psalm 22:1). David prayed those words, and Jesus cried them from the cross (Matthew 27:46). The feeling of being abandoned by God is one of the deepest kinds of suffering, and it is not new.

What do we do with it?

- **Say it to God.** The psalmists did not hide their sense of God's absence; they prayed it. That itself is an act of faith.
- **Remember what God has said.** "Never will I leave you, never will I forsake you" (Hebrews 13:5). "Can a woman forget her nursing child...? Even if she could forget, I will not forget you!" (Isaiah 49:15). Our feelings are real, but His promises are truer.
- **Look at the cross.** Jesus was truly forsaken, bearing the sins of His people, so that those who trust Him would never be. The worst abandonment has already been suffered, by Him, in our place.
- **Keep using the means of grace** even when they feel empty: reading Scripture, praying, gathering with the church, taking the Lord's Supper.
- **Ask for help.** A pastor or friend can hold up truth for you when you cannot hold it yourself.

Seasons of darkness do end. Psalm 22 begins with abandonment and ends with praise.

> **From the *Catechism of Suffering*, question 7:** Does suffering mean God has forsaken us?
>
> "No. For those in Christ, suffering is never a sign of abandonment but of Fatherly love and refinement."
>
> Matthew Statler, [suffering.catechize.ing](https://suffering.catechize.ing/questions/cos-7)
