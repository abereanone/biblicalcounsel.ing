Christians may learn true facts from careful observation of people, since all truth is God's truth. But psychological theories and therapies are human interpretations, not a second revelation, and they must be judged by Scripture rather than placed beside it as an equal authority.

## Proofs
- Psalm 19:1-4
- Romans 1:18-21
- Isaiah 8:20
- Colossians 2:8
- 2 Timothy 3:16-17

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

The "integration" view holds that God has given two books, Scripture and nature, and that psychology reads the book of nature, so the two should be combined in counseling.

There is truth here. God does reveal Himself in creation (Psalm 19:1; Romans 1:20), and Christians can learn facts from careful study: that sleep loss affects mood, for example, or that certain illnesses affect the mind.

But the argument confuses two things:

- **General revelation** is what God has made, and it is always true.
- **Psychological theories** are fallen human interpretations of what God has made. They change constantly, they contradict one another, and they usually assume a godless view of man.

As pastor and counselor Matthew Statler has argued, general revelation is real, but human theories about it are fallible interpretations, not a second revelation. When a method claims to explain why a person sins or how his heart changes, it is speaking where Scripture has already spoken, and it must submit to Scripture.

So a biblical counselor can welcome medical facts and practical wisdom, while refusing to let any theory redefine the problem, the goal or the means of change.
