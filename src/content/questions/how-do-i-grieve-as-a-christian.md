Christians grieve truly, but with hope. Jesus wept at His friend's grave, and we may weep too, while holding on to the promise that those who die in Christ will be raised.

## Proofs
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13-14
- John 11:35
- Psalm 34:18
- Revelation 21:4
- Psalm 23:4

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Paul does not tell grieving Christians not to grieve. He tells them not to "grieve like the rest, who are without hope" (1 Thessalonians 4:13).

Grief is love that has lost its object. It is not a lack of faith. "Jesus wept" (John 11:35), even though He knew He was about to raise Lazarus.

Christian grief has particular marks:

- **It is honest.** Bring your sorrow to God in prayer and lament.
- **It is hopeful.** "Since we believe that Jesus died and rose again, we also believe that God will bring with Jesus those who have fallen asleep in Him" (1 Thessalonians 4:14).
- **It takes time.** There is no schedule for grief. Anniversaries, holidays and small reminders may bring waves of sorrow for years.
- **It is shared.** God's people are called to weep with those who weep. Let others help you.
- **It looks forward.** "He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and there will be no more death or mourning or crying or pain" (Revelation 21:4).

When grief does not lift at all, or becomes despair or thoughts of not wanting to live, talk to your pastor and your doctor, and do not wait.
