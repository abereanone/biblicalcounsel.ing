Be present, listen more than you speak, help in practical ways, pray with and for them, and gently bring them the comfort of God's Word in the right season. Avoid quick answers that explain away their pain.

## Proofs
- Romans 12:15
- Job 2:13
- Galatians 6:2
- 2 Corinthians 1:3-4
- Proverbs 25:20

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Job's friends did one thing very well at first: "they sat on the ground with him for seven days and seven nights, but no one spoke a word to him because they saw how intense his suffering was" (Job 2:13). Their troubles began when they started explaining his suffering.

Some practical help:

- **Show up.** Your presence matters more than your words. "Weep with those who weep" (Romans 12:15).
- **Listen.** Ask questions and let them talk, cry or be silent.
- **Help practically.** Meals, rides, childcare, cleaning, paperwork. "Carry one another's burdens" (Galatians 6:2).
- **Pray.** With them, and on your own.
- **Share comfort, gently.** God "comforts us in all our troubles, so that we can comfort those in any trouble with the comfort we ourselves have received from God" (2 Corinthians 1:4). A short verse, read or written in a card, can carry more than a long speech.
- **Avoid clichés.** "Everything happens for a reason" or "God needed another angel" can wound. Scripture compares singing songs to a heavy heart with "vinegar poured on a wound" (Proverbs 25:20).
- **Stay.** Suffering often lasts longer than people's attention. Keep checking in.

> **From the *Catechism of Suffering*, question 49:** How should we speak to one another in times of suffering?
>
> "With gentleness and grace-filled truth, speaking words seasoned with wisdom that offer the same comfort we have received from God."
>
