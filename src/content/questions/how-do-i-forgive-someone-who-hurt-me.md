Forgiveness is a promise, made by God's grace, not to hold the offense against the person: not to bring it up to punish them, not to dwell on it, and not to let it stand between you. It is commanded, it can be hard, and it does not always mean immediate trust or the removal of consequences.

## Proofs
- Ephesians 4:32
- Colossians 3:13
- Matthew 6:14-15
- Matthew 18:21-22
- Luke 17:3-4

We forgive because we have been forgiven: "forgiving each other just as in Christ God forgave you" (Ephesians 4:32). "Forgive as the Lord forgave you" (Colossians 3:13).

What forgiveness is:

- a decision, not first a feeling;
- releasing your right to make the person pay;
- a commitment not to use the offense as a weapon, not to gossip about it, and not to brood over it.

What forgiveness is not:

- **Saying the wrong did not matter.** It did; that is why it needs forgiving.
- **Instant trust.** Trust is rebuilt over time when repentance is shown.
- **Removing all consequences.** A forgiven thief may still need to repay; a crime should still be reported to the authorities.
- **Staying in danger.** You can forgive someone and still protect yourself and others from them.

Scripture describes two sides of forgiveness. In your heart, you release bitterness and revenge before God even if the other person never repents (Mark 11:25). In the relationship, full reconciliation follows when the person repents: "if he repents, forgive him" (Luke 17:3).

If you cannot yet bring yourself to forgive, start by praying honestly: "Lord, I don't want to forgive. Help me want to."
