Because the church is where Christ has placed His people to grow: under shepherds who watch over their souls, among believers who know them, with the Word preached, the ordinances given and the body caring for its members. Counseling cut off from the church loses much of what makes it work.

## Proofs
- Ephesians 4:11-16
- Romans 15:14
- Hebrews 10:24-25
- Acts 20:28
- 1 Corinthians 12:26

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Christ gave His church "pastors and teachers" to equip the saints (Ephesians 4:11-12), so that "speaking the truth in love, we will in all things grow up into Christ Himself" and the body "builds itself up in love through the work of each individual part" (Ephesians 4:15-16).

That is a picture of counseling: truth spoken in love, by members of one body, leading to growth in Christ.

The local church offers what no counseling office can by itself:

- **Shepherds** who know the person and will give an account for his soul (Hebrews 13:17).
- **Ongoing care.** Counseling sessions end; church membership continues. Friends can check in daily, not weekly.
- **The means of grace**: the preached Word, prayer, baptism and the Lord's Supper.
- **Accountability and, when needed, loving discipline** (Matthew 18:15-17).
- **Practical help**: meals, childcare, finances, a place to belong.
- **A shared life.** "If one part suffers, every part suffers with it" (1 Corinthians 12:26).

So even when a believer receives help from a counselor outside his church, that counsel should work with his pastors, not around them.
