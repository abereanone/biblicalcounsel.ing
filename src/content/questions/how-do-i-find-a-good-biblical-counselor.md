Start with your own pastor. If you need more help, look for a counselor who is committed to the sufficiency of Scripture, accountable to a local church, sound in doctrine, wise and humble, and, ideally, who counsels freely.

## Proofs
- Proverbs 12:15
- Proverbs 15:22
- 1 Timothy 4:16
- Titus 1:9
- 1 John 4:1

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

"The way of a fool is right in his own eyes, but a wise man listens to counsel" (Proverbs 12:15). Seeking help is wise. But not all counsel is good, and Christians are told to "test the spirits" (1 John 4:1).

Where to start:

1. **Your pastor and church.** Many pastors counsel, and many churches have trained members who can help.
2. **Ask your pastor for referrals** if your church cannot help.
3. **Look for training**, such as certification with the Association of Certified Biblical Counselors or similar bodies. Certification shows training and accountability; it is not a guarantee of wisdom.

Questions to ask a possible counselor:

- Do you believe Scripture is sufficient for the problems of the soul? How do you use it?
- What church are you a member of, and who oversees your counseling?
- How do you handle confidentiality, abuse and safety?
- Will you work with my pastor?
- Is there a fee? (Counseling given freely and supported by the church is the biblical pattern.)

Be cautious of anyone who mixes Scripture with ideas that contradict it, who is not accountable to a church, who promises quick fixes, or who discourages appropriate medical care.
