Nouthetic counseling is the name Jay Adams gave to biblical counseling in the 1970s, from the Greek word for admonish. It means lovingly confronting a person with God's Word, out of concern for him, in order to bring about change.

## Proofs
- Colossians 1:28
- Romans 15:14
- Colossians 3:16
- Acts 20:31
- 1 Corinthians 4:14

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

In 1970 Jay Adams published *Competent to Counsel*, arguing that pastors and believers, equipped with Scripture, are competent to counsel, and that the church had wrongly handed the care of souls over to secular psychiatry. His work started the modern biblical counseling movement.

He took the name from the Greek verb *noutheteo*, translated "admonish" or "instruct." Paul uses it when he says believers are "able to instruct one another" (Romans 15:14), and when he describes his own ministry: "admonishing and teaching everyone with all wisdom" (Colossians 1:28). For three years in Ephesus he "never stopped warning each of you night and day with tears" (Acts 20:31).

Adams saw three elements in the word:

1. **Confrontation with the Word**, speaking to a real problem rather than around it;
2. **Concern for the person**, as a father warns his children (1 Corinthians 4:14);
3. **Change**, aiming at a life that pleases God.

Later biblical counselors have emphasized more fully the role of suffering, the heart's desires and the comfort of the gospel, but they stand on the foundation Adams laid: the sufficiency of Scripture and the ministry of the church.
