By entrusting himself to God, as Christ did: bringing his pain honestly to the Father, continuing to do good, refusing bitterness and revenge, and waiting in hope for God's deliverance.

## Proofs
- 1 Peter 4:19
- 1 Peter 2:21-23
- Psalm 62:8
- Romans 12:12
- James 1:2-4

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Peter gives a simple summary: "those who suffer according to God's will should entrust their souls to their faithful Creator and continue to do good" (1 Peter 4:19).

The model is Jesus Himself: "When they heaped abuse on Him, He did not retaliate; when He suffered, He made no threats, but entrusted Himself to Him who judges justly" (1 Peter 2:23).

That pattern has several parts:

1. **Honesty with God.** "Pour out your hearts before Him" (Psalm 62:8). Faith is not pretending.
2. **Trust.** Hand yourself, your circumstances and your future over to God again and again, especially when you cannot see what He is doing.
3. **Obedience.** "Continue to do good." Suffering does not suspend our callings; it often becomes the place where faithfulness shines.
4. **No revenge.** When others cause our suffering, we leave judgment to God.
5. **Hope.** "Be joyful in hope, patient in affliction, persistent in prayer" (Romans 12:12).

This is not a technique for feeling better quickly. It is a way of walking with God through the dark.

> **From the *Catechism of Suffering*, question 29:** What example did Christ leave for us in suffering?
>
> "He entrusted Himself to the One who judges justly, overcame evil with steadfast obedience, and set the pattern for us to follow in obedience to God, by not returning evil for evil, but doing good."
>
