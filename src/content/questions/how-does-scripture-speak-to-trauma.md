Scripture takes terrible suffering seriously and does not ask victims to minimize it. It shows a God who sees, a Savior who suffered, and a hope that is stronger than the memory of what happened, and it calls the church to walk patiently with those who carry such wounds.

## Proofs
- Psalm 34:18
- Hebrews 4:15
- Isaiah 53:3
- 2 Corinthians 4:8-10
- Psalm 56:8

Trauma is the lasting effect of overwhelming events: violence, abuse, war, accidents, sudden loss. People who have been through such things may struggle with vivid memories, fear, sleeplessness, being easily startled, numbness, shame or anger, sometimes for years.

Scripture is full of people who lived through horrors: Joseph sold by his brothers, David hunted by Saul, Jeremiah in the pit, Paul beaten and shipwrecked. It does not tell them to pretend. And it speaks to sufferers with real comfort:

- **God sees.** "Put my tears in Your bottle—are they not in Your book?" (Psalm 56:8).
- **God is near.** "Yahweh is near to the brokenhearted" (Psalm 34:18).
- **Christ understands.** He was "a man of sorrows, acquainted with grief" (Isaiah 53:3) and is able "to sympathize with our weaknesses" (Hebrews 4:15).
- **Suffering does not have the last word.** "We are hard pressed on all sides, but not crushed" (2 Corinthians 4:8).

Wise care is patient. It makes sure the person is safe, encourages medical help for sleep and physical symptoms, does not force anyone to retell what happened before they are ready, and slowly helps them interpret their story in the light of God's character and promises.
