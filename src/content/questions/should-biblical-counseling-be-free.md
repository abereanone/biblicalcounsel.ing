Yes. Counseling from God's Word is ministry of the Word, and Christ commands that His ministry be given freely. Churches and believers should support counselors, so that no one is charged for counsel or turned away because they cannot pay.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- 1 Corinthians 9:18
- Galatians 6:6
- 2 Corinthians 2:17
- Proverbs 3:27

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

If counseling is ministry of the Word, as biblical counselors rightly say, then Jesus' command applies to it: "Freely you have received; freely give" (Matthew 10:8). We would not charge someone to hear a sermon or receive a hospital visit from their pastor. Counseling is the same ministry, applied to one person.

That does not mean counselors are not worthy of support. "The one who receives instruction in the word must share in all good things with his instructor" (Galatians 6:6). The difference is in how support comes:

- **Not this:** a counselor charging the counselee by the hour, so that help from God's Word depends on the person's ability to pay.
- **But this:** the church, and other believers who share in the work, supporting the counselor so that counsel can be given freely to all.

Paul's reward was "that in preaching the gospel I may offer it free of charge" (1 Corinthians 9:18). Counselors can share that reward.

Many churches already counsel freely through pastors and trained members. Counseling centers can be supported by partner churches and donors rather than fees. The Association of Certified Biblical Counselors currently leaves fees to each counselor's judgment, while affirming that the gospel should be offered without price; many have urged that the standard go further.
