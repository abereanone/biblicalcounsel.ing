It is Scripture's pattern for change: put off a specific sinful habit, be renewed in your thinking, and put on the specific godly habit that replaces it. Stopping a sin is never enough; something righteous must take its place.

## Proofs
- Ephesians 4:22-24
- Ephesians 4:25-32
- Colossians 3:8-14

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Paul describes change as changing clothes: "put off your former way of life, your old self... be renewed in the spirit of your minds; and to put on the new self, created to be like God in true righteousness and holiness" (Ephesians 4:22-24).

Then he gives examples, and each one has a replacement:

| Put off | Put on |
|---|---|
| Lying | Speaking the truth (Ephesians 4:25) |
| Stealing | Working and giving to those in need (Ephesians 4:28) |
| Unwholesome talk | Words that build up (Ephesians 4:29) |
| Bitterness, rage, anger | Kindness, tenderness, forgiveness (Ephesians 4:31-32) |

The thief is not finished when he stops stealing. He must start working and giving. The angry man is not finished when he stops yelling. He must learn tenderness.

In counseling, this becomes practical: name the specific sin, understand the desire behind it, find what Scripture commands in its place, and plan concrete ways to practice the new habit, especially at the moments when the old one usually wins.
