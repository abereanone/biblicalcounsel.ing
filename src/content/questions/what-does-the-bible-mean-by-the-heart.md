In Scripture the heart is the inner person: the center of what we think, desire, love, trust and choose. Our words and actions flow out of it, so real change must reach the heart.

## Proofs
- Proverbs 4:23
- Mark 7:21-23
- Luke 6:45
- Jeremiah 17:9
- Hebrews 4:12

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

When the Bible speaks of the heart, it rarely means feelings alone. It means the whole inner self: the mind that thinks, the will that chooses, the affections that love, the faith that trusts.

That is why Scripture says, "Guard your heart with all diligence, for from it flow springs of life" (Proverbs 4:23). Jesus taught that sinful actions are not caused merely by circumstances: "from within the hearts of men come evil thoughts, sexual immorality, theft, murder, adultery, greed..." (Mark 7:21-22). And "out of the overflow of the heart the mouth speaks" (Luke 6:45).

This matters for counseling:

- **Behavior is fruit, not the root.** Changing outward habits without addressing the heart is like cutting the tops off weeds.
- **The heart is deceitful.** "The heart is deceitful above all things and beyond cure. Who can understand it?" (Jeremiah 17:9). We often do not know our own motives until God's Word exposes them.
- **God's Word reaches it.** Scripture "judges the thoughts and intentions of the heart" (Hebrews 4:12).

So a biblical counselor asks not only *what did you do?* but *what did you want, fear, believe and love when you did it?*
