God does not explain every trial, but He tells us that He rules over all of them, that He works them together for the good of those who love Him, and that He uses them to make us like Christ and prepare us for glory.

## Proofs
- Romans 8:28-29
- Genesis 50:20
- Hebrews 12:10-11
- 2 Corinthians 4:17
- Psalm 119:71

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Suffering entered the world through sin, and all creation now groans under it (Romans 8:20-22). Believers are not exempt. But for God's children, suffering is never random or wasted.

Scripture gives several purposes:

- **For our good.** "God works all things together for the good of those who love Him" (Romans 8:28). That good is defined in the next verse: being "conformed to the image of His Son" (Romans 8:29).
- **For our holiness.** God disciplines us "for our good, so that we may share in His holiness," and it "yields a harvest of righteousness and peace" (Hebrews 12:10-11).
- **For others' good.** Joseph told his brothers, "what you intended against me for evil, God intended for good" (Genesis 50:20).
- **For glory to come.** "Our light and momentary affliction is producing for us an eternal weight of glory that is far beyond comparison" (2 Corinthians 4:17).
- **To teach us God's Word.** "It was good for me to be afflicted, that I might learn Your statutes" (Psalm 119:71).

We will not always know why a particular trial has come. We can know who sent it, that He loves us, and that He is not finished.

> **From the *Catechism of Suffering*, question 6:** Why does God allow His people to suffer?
>
> "He allows suffering to display His glory, test and deepen our faith, and conform us to the image of His Son."
>
> Matthew Statler, [suffering.catechize.ing](https://suffering.catechize.ing/questions/cos-6)
