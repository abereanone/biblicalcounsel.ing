The goal is that the person being counseled would glorify God by becoming more like Jesus Christ: trusting Him, obeying Him and loving God and neighbor, whether or not every difficulty goes away.

## Proofs
- Romans 8:29
- 2 Corinthians 3:18
- Colossians 1:28
- 1 Corinthians 10:31
- Matthew 22:37-39

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Relief from pain is a good thing to pray for, and biblical counselors want people to experience peace, joy and restored relationships. But relief is not the highest goal.

God's purpose for His people is that they be "conformed to the image of His Son" (Romans 8:29). Paul labored "so that we may present everyone perfect in Christ" (Colossians 1:28). Believers "are being transformed into His image with intensifying glory" (2 Corinthians 3:18).

That goal changes counseling in practical ways:

- **Success is not measured only by feelings.** A woman whose husband never changes may still grow beautifully in faith, patience and love.
- **Hard circumstances are not wasted.** Suffering can be the very place where Christlikeness grows.
- **The counselor serves God's agenda, not only the counselee's.** He helps the person want what God wants for him.
- **Love is the fruit.** Jesus summarized the law as loving God with all your heart and loving your neighbor as yourself (Matthew 22:37-39).

Seeking Christlikeness first does not make people miserable. It is the path to the deepest joy there is.
