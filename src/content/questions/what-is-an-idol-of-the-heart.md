An idol of the heart is anything we love, trust or serve in God's place, often a good thing turned into a ruling thing. Idols promise life and deliver slavery.

## Proofs
- Ezekiel 14:3
- Colossians 3:5
- Romans 1:25
- 1 John 5:21
- Jonah 2:8

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

God said of Israel's elders, "these men have set up idols in their hearts" (Ezekiel 14:3). They did not need statues to be idolaters. Paul called greed "idolatry" (Colossians 3:5), and John ended his letter, "Little children, keep yourselves from idols" (1 John 5:21).

An idol is anything that takes God's place in our hearts. It is often a good gift turned into a god:

- **Approval** becomes a god when we must have people's good opinion.
- **Comfort** becomes a god when we cannot bear any discomfort.
- **Control** becomes a god when we must have things our way.
- **Success, a spouse, children, health, pleasure**: each can become the thing we cannot live without.

You can often find an idol by what you worry about most, what makes you angriest when it is threatened, and what you run to for relief.

The answer is not merely to remove the idol but to replace it. We exchange "the truth of God for a lie" (Romans 1:25); repentance exchanges it back. As God becomes our treasure, His good gifts can return to their proper place, as gifts.
