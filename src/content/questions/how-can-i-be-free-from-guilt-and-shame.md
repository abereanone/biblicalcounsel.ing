Real guilt is removed by confessing sin to God and trusting Christ, who bore it. Shame, whether from our own sin or from what others did to us, is answered by Christ, who bore our shame and clothes us in His righteousness. In Him there is no condemnation.

## Proofs
- 1 John 1:9
- Romans 8:1
- Psalm 32:1-5
- Hebrews 12:2
- Isaiah 54:4

**Guilt** is the true condition of having sinned. **Shame** is the painful sense of being exposed, unclean or unworthy. They often come together, but not always: victims of abuse often feel deep shame for what others did to them.

For guilt, the answer is confession and forgiveness:

- "When I kept silent, my bones became brittle from my groaning all day long" (Psalm 32:3). Hiding sin makes it heavier.
- "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9).
- "There is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" (Romans 8:1).

For shame, the answer is who Christ is and what He did:

- Jesus "endured the cross, scorning its shame" (Hebrews 12:2). He took the shame of the cross on Himself.
- God promises His people, "Do not be afraid, for you will not be put to shame" (Isaiah 54:4).
- If you have been sinned against, the uncleanness belongs to the one who sinned, not to you.

Ongoing guilt after confession is often a matter of not believing God's promise of forgiveness. Bring that unbelief to Him too, and let a pastor or friend remind you of the gospel.
