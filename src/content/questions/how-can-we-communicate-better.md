Speak the truth, speak it in love, speak only what builds up, listen before answering, and choose gentle words. Good communication begins with a heart that wants the other person's good more than its own way.

## Proofs
- Ephesians 4:15
- Ephesians 4:25
- Ephesians 4:29
- James 1:19
- Proverbs 18:13

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Paul gives four rules for communication in Ephesians 4:

1. **Be honest.** "Each of you must put off falsehood and speak truthfully to his neighbor" (Ephesians 4:25). Do not hide, exaggerate or manipulate.
2. **Deal with problems quickly.** "Do not let the sun set upon your anger" (Ephesians 4:26).
3. **Attack the problem, not the person.** "Let no unwholesome talk come out of your mouths, but only what is helpful for building up the one in need" (Ephesians 4:29).
4. **Act, not just react.** Put off bitterness and harsh words; put on kindness and forgiveness (Ephesians 4:31-32).

To these add the wisdom of James and Proverbs:

- "Everyone should be quick to listen, slow to speak, and slow to anger" (James 1:19).
- "He who answers a matter before he hears it—this is folly and disgrace to him" (Proverbs 18:13).

Practical habits: set aside regular time to talk without screens; repeat back what you heard before responding; avoid "always" and "never"; ask "What would help?"; and pray together, even briefly, every day.
