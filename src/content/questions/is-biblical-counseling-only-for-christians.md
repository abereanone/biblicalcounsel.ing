Biblical counselors gladly help unbelievers, but the help begins with the gospel. Without a new heart from Christ, a person can make outward changes but cannot truly please God or change from the inside.

## Proofs
- Ephesians 2:1-5
- 2 Corinthians 5:17
- Ezekiel 36:26-27
- John 3:3
- Matthew 11:28-30

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Scripture describes every person outside of Christ as "dead in your trespasses and sins" (Ephesians 2:1). Counsel that tells a spiritually dead man to try harder will only frustrate him, or make him proud of a reformed life that still has no room for God.

So when an unbeliever comes for help, a biblical counselor will:

- **listen and care** about the real problem, whether it is a failing marriage, grief, fear or addiction;
- **explain the gospel** clearly: that God is holy, that we have sinned, that Christ died and rose for sinners, and that He calls all to repent and believe;
- **offer practical wisdom** where it helps, such as stopping a destructive habit or seeking safety, while being honest that lasting change requires a new heart;
- **invite** the person to come to Christ: "Come to Me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28).

"If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation" (2 Corinthians 5:17). That is the beginning of real counseling.
