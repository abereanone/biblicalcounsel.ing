That decision belongs to the person and his doctor. Medicine is a good gift of God's common grace and may relieve some physical symptoms, but no medication can change the heart, forgive sin or give hope in God. Never stop or change a medication without your doctor.

## Proofs
- 1 Timothy 5:23
- Luke 5:31
- Psalm 42:5
- 2 Corinthians 4:16-18

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Christians have long used medicine as a gift of God. Jesus said, "It is not the healthy who need a doctor, but the sick" (Luke 5:31), and Paul recommended a remedy for Timothy's stomach (1 Timothy 5:23).

Biblical counselors usually encourage a few principles:

- **Get a good medical evaluation.** Some depression and anxiety has clear physical causes, and those should be treated.
- **Decide with your doctor, not alone.** Starting, stopping or changing psychiatric medication can be dangerous without medical supervision. Do not stop a medication because of something you read, including on this site.
- **Be realistic about what medicine can do.** It may ease some symptoms, such as sleeplessness or an overwhelming sense of dread, which can make it easier to think and pray. It cannot change what you love, forgive your sins, heal a broken relationship or give you hope in God.
- **Do not let medicine replace ministry.** Whether or not you take medication, you still need the Word, prayer, the church and the hope of the gospel: "Put your hope in God, for I will yet praise Him" (Psalm 42:5).

Christians disagree about how often these medicines help. They should not disagree that every sufferer needs Christ.
