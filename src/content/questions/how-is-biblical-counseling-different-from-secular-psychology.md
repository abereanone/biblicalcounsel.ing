They begin from different answers to the deepest questions: what a person is, what is wrong with him, what he needs, and how he changes. Secular approaches answer without God; biblical counseling answers from God's Word.

## Proofs
- Colossians 2:8
- Psalm 1:1-2
- Jeremiah 17:9
- Romans 12:2
- Ezekiel 36:26-27

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Every approach to counseling, whether it says so or not, answers a few questions:

| Question | Secular answer (broadly) | Biblical answer |
|---|---|---|
| What is a person? | A product of biology and environment | A creature made in God's image, body and soul |
| What is the problem? | Unmet needs, faulty thinking, trauma, imbalance | Sin within and suffering without, in a fallen world, before a holy God |
| What is the goal? | Feeling better, functioning well, self-fulfillment | Glorifying God and being made like Christ |
| How does change happen? | Technique, insight, the right environment | Repentance and faith, the Spirit working through the Word |

Secular counselors often observe real patterns in human behavior, and some of their observations are accurate. But observations are always interpreted by a worldview, and a worldview without God will misread the heart. Paul warns, "See to it that no one takes you captive through philosophy and empty deception, which are based on human tradition... rather than on Christ" (Colossians 2:8).

A biblical counselor is not afraid of facts. He is careful about frameworks.
