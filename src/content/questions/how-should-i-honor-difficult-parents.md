Honoring your parents is commanded for all of life, but it does not mean obeying them as an adult, approving their sin or staying under abuse. It means treating them with respect, caring for them, speaking well of them where you truthfully can, and forgiving them, while setting wise limits where needed.

## Proofs
- Exodus 20:12
- Ephesians 6:1-3
- Proverbs 23:22
- 1 Timothy 5:4
- Romans 12:18

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

"Honor your father and mother" (Exodus 20:12) has no age limit. But it looks different at different stages of life:

- **Children obey** their parents "in the Lord" (Ephesians 6:1).
- **Adult children honor** their parents, but they have left father and mother (Genesis 2:24) and are responsible to God for their own decisions.

Honoring difficult parents can include:

- **Respectful speech**, even when you disagree, and refusing to mock or slander them.
- **Practical care**, especially as they age (1 Timothy 5:4).
- **Forgiveness** for past wrongs, released to God even if they never apologize.
- **Truthfulness.** Honor does not require pretending that wrongs did not happen.

Honor does not require:

- **Obeying sinful demands** (Acts 5:29).
- **Staying in harm's way.** If a parent is abusive or dangerous, you may set firm limits on contact to protect yourself and your children.
- **Giving up your marriage's priority.** Your spouse comes first.

"If it is possible on your part, live at peace with everyone" (Romans 12:18). Sometimes peace takes distance. Keep praying for them, and keep the door open to reconciliation if they repent.
