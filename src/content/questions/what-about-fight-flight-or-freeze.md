The body's alarm reactions to danger are part of how God made us, and they are not sinful in themselves. But what we do next comes from the heart, and Scripture shows that in the same moment of terror one person can lash out, another can run, and Christ can entrust Himself to the Father.

## Proofs
- Mark 14:33-36
- John 18:10-11
- Mark 14:50
- 1 Peter 2:23
- Psalm 56:3-4

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

"Fight, flight or freeze" describes how the body reacts to sudden danger: a racing heart, a rush of strength, an urge to run, or a sense of being stuck. These reactions are real and often involuntary. A soldier who jumps at a loud noise has not sinned.

But the grid cannot explain everything. Matthew Statler has pointed out that the night of Jesus' arrest shows three very different responses to the same danger:

- **Peter fought.** He drew a sword and cut off a servant's ear (John 18:10). His zeal was real, but it was not obedience, and Jesus told him to put the sword away.
- **The disciples fled.** "Then everyone deserted Him and fled" (Mark 14:50). Fear of men won.
- **Jesus was silent and steady.** He was "deeply troubled and distressed" (Mark 14:33), yet He prayed, "not what I will, but what You will" (Mark 14:36), and "entrusted Himself to Him who judges justly" (1 Peter 2:23).

So the body's alarm is not the whole story. In the moment of fear, the heart still trusts something. "When I am afraid, I put my trust in You" (Psalm 56:3). Counseling helps a person learn, slowly and with grace, to bring the alarm to God.

> **From the *Catechism of Suffering*, question 32:** How does suffering reveal our hearts?
>
> "Suffering exposes what we love, trust, and treasure, revealing the desires and conflicts within that show where our hope truly lies."
>
> Matthew Statler, [suffering.catechize.ing](https://suffering.catechize.ing/questions/cos-32)
