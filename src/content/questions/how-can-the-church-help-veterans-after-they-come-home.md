By welcoming them, investing in them, and giving them a place to serve. Many veterans struggle when the mission ends; the church can help them find their hope in Christ rather than in country or service, resist bitterness and isolation, and take up a new mission in family, church and community.

## Proofs
- Psalm 146:3
- Hebrews 12:15
- Ecclesiastes 4:9-10
- 1 Samuel 22:1-2
- 2 Timothy 2:3

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Veterans come home changed. Some carry injuries and memories; many miss the purpose, discipline and brotherhood of service. Matthew Statler, a pastor, biblical counselor and Army veteran who served in Iraq, has written to fellow veterans that the mission did not end when they came home. It changed.

Ways the church can help:

- **Welcome them personally.** Learn their stories, invite them to your table, and keep inviting. "Two are better than one... if one falls down, his companion can lift him up" (Ecclesiastes 4:9-10).
- **Point their hope to Christ.** "Put not your trust in princes, in mortal man, who cannot save" (Psalm 146:3). Country, unit and politics cannot bear the weight of a soul's hope.
- **Guard against bitterness and self-pity.** Real wrongs and real losses can turn into a root that defiles many (Hebrews 12:15). Help them bring grievances to God and keep moving forward.
- **Give them brothers.** Statler points to David's men in the cave of Adullam, "distressed or indebted or discontented" men whom David led (1 Samuel 22:1-2). The church can be that kind of company: honest, loyal and on mission.
- **Give them a mission.** Strengthen their marriages, encourage them to disciple younger men, and give them real work in the church. Many already know what it is to endure hardship "like a good soldier" (2 Timothy 2:3).
- **Watch for crisis.** If a veteran talks about not wanting to live, act at once. In the United States, call or text 988 and press 1 for the Veterans Crisis Line, or call 911.
