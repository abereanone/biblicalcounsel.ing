Every mature Christian is called to counsel others with God's Word in ordinary life. Pastors bear special responsibility, and some believers are trained for more formal counseling, but the ministry of the Word belongs to the whole church.

## Proofs
- Romans 15:14
- Colossians 3:16
- Galatians 6:1-2
- Hebrews 3:13
- Titus 2:3-5

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Paul told an ordinary congregation, "you yourselves are full of goodness, brimming with knowledge, and able to instruct one another" (Romans 15:14). Believers are to "teach and admonish one another with all wisdom" (Colossians 3:16), to restore those caught in sin (Galatians 6:1), and to "exhort one another daily" (Hebrews 3:13).

So counseling is not reserved for professionals. A friend who opens the Scriptures with you over coffee, an older woman who teaches a young mother (Titus 2:3-5), a father who talks with his son: all of these are counseling.

At the same time:

- **Pastors** are charged to shepherd the flock and will give an account for souls (Acts 20:28; Hebrews 13:17), so they carry a particular responsibility for counsel.
- **Trained counselors** can help with more complex or long-standing problems. Training sharpens wisdom; it does not replace the Spirit or the Word.
- **Wisdom and character matter more than credentials.** A counselor must be walking with God himself (Galatians 6:1).

Biblical counselors have generally held that men should counsel men and women should counsel women, except where a pastor or husband is also present, to protect everyone involved and keep the ministry above reproach.
