Begin with your own heart: what you wanted that you did not get. Take the log out of your own eye first, confess your part specifically, listen to understand, speak the truth in love, and forgive as Christ forgave you.

## Proofs
- James 4:1-2
- Matthew 7:3-5
- Ephesians 4:25-32
- Colossians 3:12-14
- Proverbs 15:1

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

"What causes conflicts and quarrels among you? Don't they come from the passions at war within you?" (James 4:1). Most marital conflict is not really about the dishes, money or schedules. It is about desires (for comfort, respect, control, appreciation) that have become demands.

A pattern for working through conflict:

1. **Pray first**, and cool down if you need to. But do not let it fester (Ephesians 4:26).
2. **Look at yourself first.** "First take the beam out of your own eye, and then you will see clearly to remove the speck from your brother's eye" (Matthew 7:5). What did you want? How did you respond?
3. **Confess specifically.** Not "I'm sorry if you were hurt," but "I was wrong to speak to you with contempt. Will you forgive me?"
4. **Listen to understand.** Ask questions before answering (Proverbs 18:13).
5. **Speak the truth in love** (Ephesians 4:15), gently: "A gentle answer turns away wrath" (Proverbs 15:1).
6. **Forgive**, and do not bring it up later as a weapon (Ephesians 4:32).
7. **Solve the problem together**, as teammates against the problem rather than opponents.

"And over all these virtues put on love, which is the bond of perfect unity" (Colossians 3:14). If conflicts keep escalating, ask your pastor for help early, not as a last resort.
