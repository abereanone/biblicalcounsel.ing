Scripture describes deep sorrow and despair in faithful people like David, Elijah, Jeremiah and Paul, and it does not condemn them for it. It points them to God's presence and to hope, while also caring for the body. Anyone in a dark season should talk to a pastor and see a doctor.

## Proofs
- Psalm 42:5
- Psalm 88
- 1 Kings 19:4-8
- 2 Corinthians 1:8-9
- Psalm 34:18

The word "depression" covers many things, from a season of heavy sadness to a severe illness. Scripture does not use the modern term, but it describes the experience vividly. "Why are you downcast, O my soul? Why the unease within me?" (Psalm 42:5). Psalm 88 ends in darkness. Elijah wanted to die (1 Kings 19:4). Paul despaired of life (2 Corinthians 1:8).

What helps:

- **Care for the body.** God's first help for Elijah was sleep and food. See a doctor: thyroid problems, vitamin deficiencies, sleep disorders, medications and other illnesses can cause or deepen depression.
- **Speak to your soul.** The psalmist does not only listen to his feelings; he answers them: "Put your hope in God, for I will yet praise Him" (Psalm 42:5).
- **Keep praying, even in the dark.** Lament is faith speaking honestly.
- **Look for causes without assuming them.** Sometimes there is unconfessed sin, bitterness, grief or fear underneath; sometimes the cause is mostly physical; often it is both. A wise counselor will help you sort this out gently.
- **Stay connected.** Isolation deepens darkness. Let your church carry you.
- **Do small, faithful things.** When you cannot do much, do the next small thing in obedience: get up, read a psalm, eat, take a walk, call a friend.

If depression ever includes thoughts of ending your life, get help immediately: call or text 988 in the United States, or 911.
