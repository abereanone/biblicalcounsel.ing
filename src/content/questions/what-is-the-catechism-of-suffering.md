It is a booklet of sixty short questions and answers on affliction by Matthew Statler, a pastor and biblical counselor, freely given and online at suffering.catechize.ing. Memorized and prayed over, its answers help sufferers speak truth to their own hearts when pain makes it hard to think.

## Proofs
- Psalm 119:50
- Psalm 42:5
- Deuteronomy 6:6-7
- Colossians 3:16

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Suffering clouds the mind. Truths we believed easily in quiet seasons can feel far away in the hospital, at the graveside or in the long night. A catechism helps because it is short, memorable and grounded in Scripture. Each answer gives the heart something solid to hold.

The *Catechism of Suffering* moves through six sections: the reality of suffering, the God who reigns, the God who draws near, the purpose of suffering, the comfort of the saints, and the glory to come. Every answer is followed by Scripture proofs.

Ways to use it:

- **One question a day.** Read it in the morning, then return to it through the day.
- **Memorize.** "This is my comfort in affliction, that Your promise has given me life" (Psalm 119:50). What is stored in the heart is available when the book is not.
- **Pray the answers back to God** in your own words.
- **In families,** with children asking and parents answering, as Israel was told to talk of God's words at home and along the road (Deuteronomy 6:6-7).
- **In counseling,** as homework between meetings, or as a shared language between counselor and counselee.
- **Pass it on.** The author gives it away freely and asks only that it be shared with others who suffer.

Many answers on this site, especially in the Suffering and Hope section, close with the matching answer from the catechism.
