The hope of glory: that the God who raised Jesus will raise us, that our present suffering is producing something eternal, and that Christ will make all things new. This hope does not disappoint, because God's love has already been poured into our hearts.

## Proofs
- Romans 8:18
- Romans 5:3-5
- 1 Peter 1:6-7
- Revelation 21:3-5
- 2 Corinthians 4:17

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

"I consider that our present sufferings are not comparable to the glory that will be revealed in us" (Romans 8:18). Paul was not minimizing suffering. He had been beaten, imprisoned, stoned and shipwrecked. He was comparing it with something greater.

Christian hope is not wishful thinking. It rests on things God has already done and promised:

- **Christ is risen.** His resurrection is the firstfruits of ours.
- **Suffering is producing something.** "Suffering produces perseverance; perseverance, character; and character, hope. And hope does not disappoint us, because God has poured out His love into our hearts through the Holy Spirit" (Romans 5:3-5).
- **Faith is being refined.** Trials prove faith "more precious than gold" (1 Peter 1:7).
- **God will dwell with His people.** "He will wipe away every tear from their eyes" (Revelation 21:4), and "Behold, I make all things new" (Revelation 21:5).

In counseling, hope is not a final paragraph tacked on to practical advice. It is the ground on which everything else stands. People can endure almost anything if they know it will end well, and in Christ it will.
