A counselor listens carefully to understand the person and the problem, gives hope from God's promises, opens the Scriptures to show how God sees the situation, calls for specific steps of faith and obedience, and follows up as the person grows.

## Proofs
- Proverbs 18:13
- Proverbs 20:5
- James 1:22
- Romans 15:4
- 1 Thessalonians 5:14

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Every counseling relationship is different, but the work usually includes:

1. **Listening.** "He who answers a matter before he hears it—this is folly and disgrace to him" (Proverbs 18:13). A good counselor asks questions, gathers facts and draws out the heart.
2. **Giving hope.** People come discouraged. Scripture was written "so that, through endurance and the encouragement of the Scriptures, we might have hope" (Romans 15:4).
3. **Understanding biblically.** Together, counselor and counselee look at the problem through Scripture's categories: what is suffering, what is sin, what the person is trusting and wanting.
4. **Instruction.** The counselor teaches what God says about the situation and the promises that apply.
5. **Practice.** Change happens between meetings, not only in them. Counselors often give homework: Bible reading, prayer, specific acts of obedience, conversations to have. "Be doers of the word, and not hearers only" (James 1:22).
6. **Follow-up.** Progress is reviewed, obstacles discussed, and the counselee is connected more deeply to his church.

Counseling might last a few meetings or many months. The aim is always for the person to need the counselor less and the Lord more.
