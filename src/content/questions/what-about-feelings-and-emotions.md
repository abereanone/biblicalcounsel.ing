Emotions are part of how God made us, and Scripture is full of them. They are real but not always reliable: they reveal what we believe and love, and they should be brought to God, examined by His Word, and not allowed to rule us.

## Proofs
- Psalm 42:5
- Ephesians 4:26
- John 11:35
- Psalm 62:8
- Philippians 4:6-7

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Jesus wept at Lazarus' tomb (John 11:35). The Psalms are full of joy, grief, fear, anger and longing. Christianity does not ask us to stop feeling.

But emotions are not neutral or self-explaining. They grow out of the heart, from what we believe, value and want. Fear tells us something we love is threatened. Anger tells us something we want is being blocked. That makes emotions useful in counseling: they point to the heart.

Scripture shows how to handle them:

- **Speak to God honestly.** "Pour out your hearts before Him" (Psalm 62:8).
- **Question your feelings with truth.** "Why are you downcast, O my soul? Why the unease within me? Put your hope in God" (Psalm 42:5). The psalmist talks *to* his feelings instead of only listening to them.
- **Do not let them lead you into sin.** "Be angry, yet do not sin" (Ephesians 4:26).
- **Bring them to God in prayer**, and receive His peace, "which surpasses all understanding" (Philippians 4:7).

Feelings make good servants and poor masters.
