Grow in the Word and in godliness first. Then learn from your pastors, read sound books on biblical counseling, take training courses, watch experienced counselors, and practice under supervision. The goal is not technique but wisdom.

## Proofs
- 2 Timothy 2:2
- 2 Timothy 3:16-17
- Romans 15:14
- Galatians 6:1
- James 3:1

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Paul told Timothy to entrust what he had learned "to faithful men who will be qualified to teach others as well" (2 Timothy 2:2). Counselors are trained the same way: by faithful people, in the context of the church.

A path for growth:

1. **Know your Bible.** Read all of it, regularly. Learn sound doctrine. A counselor who does not know the Word well cannot apply it well.
2. **Walk with God.** "Watch yourself, or you also may be tempted" (Galatians 6:1). Character matters more than knowledge.
3. **Ask your pastors** how you can help in your church's care, and learn from them.
4. **Read.** Classic and recent books on biblical counseling, and especially books on how Scripture speaks to particular struggles.
5. **Take training.** Many churches and ministries offer biblical counseling courses, some of them free online.
6. **Observe and be supervised.** Sit in (with permission) on counseling, and have your own counseling reviewed by someone more experienced.
7. **Stay humble.** "Not many of you should become teachers, my brothers, because you know that we who teach will be judged more strictly" (James 3:1).

Every Christian can learn to bring God's Word wisely and lovingly to a friend. Formal training helps those called to do more.
