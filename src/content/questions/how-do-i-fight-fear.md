By remembering who God is and that He is with you, by bringing your fears to Him, and by fearing Him more than anything else. The fear of the Lord drives out lesser fears.

## Proofs
- Isaiah 41:10
- Psalm 56:3-4
- Matthew 10:28
- Psalm 34:4
- Psalm 23:4

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

"Do not fear" is one of the most repeated commands in Scripture, and it almost always comes with a reason: *I am with you.* "Do not fear, for I am with you; do not be afraid, for I am your God. I will strengthen you; I will surely help you" (Isaiah 41:10).

Fear is not always sin. Some fear is wise. But fear becomes a problem when it rules us and keeps us from trusting and obeying God.

Scripture's answers to fear:

- **Trust in the moment of fear.** "When I am afraid, I put my trust in You" (Psalm 56:3). David did not wait until he stopped feeling afraid.
- **Remember God's presence.** "I will fear no evil, for You are with me" (Psalm 23:4).
- **Seek the Lord.** "I sought Yahweh, and He answered me; He delivered me from all my fears" (Psalm 34:4).
- **Fear God rightly.** "Do not be afraid of those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul. Instead, fear the One who can destroy both soul and body in hell" (Matthew 10:28). The one who reverences God, and knows himself loved by Him, has nothing else ultimately to fear.

A helpful practice: write down your fear, then write down what God has promised that speaks to it, and pray through the two together.
