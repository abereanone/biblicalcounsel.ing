Because change happens in daily life, not only in the counseling room. Homework helps the counselee apply God's Word between meetings: reading and meditating on Scripture, praying, practicing new habits and taking concrete steps of obedience.

## Proofs
- James 1:22-25
- Philippians 4:9
- 1 Timothy 4:7
- Hebrews 5:14

"Be doers of the word, and not hearers only. Otherwise, you are deceiving yourselves" (James 1:22). A counseling session can give understanding, but obedience happens on Tuesday afternoon when the familiar temptation comes.

Paul told the Philippians, "Whatever you have learned or received or heard from me, or seen in me—put it into practice" (Philippians 4:9). He told Timothy, "train yourself for godliness" (1 Timothy 4:7). Maturity belongs to those "who by constant use have trained their senses to distinguish good from evil" (Hebrews 5:14).

Typical homework includes:

- **Scripture**: reading, studying or memorizing passages that speak to the struggle;
- **Prayer**: specific prayers, such as praying a psalm of lament or praying for someone you resent;
- **Journaling**: recording thoughts, temptations and responses to discover patterns;
- **Put-off and put-on assignments**: specific actions to replace sinful habits;
- **Relational steps**: a conversation to have, a confession to make, an act of kindness;
- **Church involvement**: connecting with people and ministries.

Good homework is specific, achievable and tied to the person's actual life. It is reviewed at the next meeting, with grace for struggles and joy in progress.
