A faithful counselor keeps confidences and does not gossip. But confidentiality has biblical limits: a counselor cannot keep secret a danger to life, the abuse of a child or vulnerable person, a crime that must be reported, or unrepentant sin that requires the church's care. A good counselor explains these limits at the start.

## Proofs
- Proverbs 11:13
- Proverbs 20:19
- Romans 13:3-4
- Matthew 18:15-17
- Galatians 6:1

"A gossip reveals a secret, but a trustworthy person keeps a confidence" (Proverbs 11:13). People cannot open their hearts to someone they cannot trust. A biblical counselor guards what he hears.

But Scripture does not promise absolute secrecy, and wise counselors explain the limits before counseling begins:

- **Danger to life.** If someone intends to harm himself or others, the counselor must act to protect life.
- **Abuse and crimes.** The abuse of children or vulnerable adults must be reported to civil authorities, who are God's servants for justice (Romans 13:4). In many places the law requires it.
- **Church discipline.** If a church member persists in serious, unrepentant sin, Jesus' instructions may require involving others in the church (Matthew 18:15-17).
- **Getting help.** A counselor may need to consult a pastor or supervisor, usually with the counselee's knowledge.

Within those limits, a good counselor will share information only as widely as necessary, only with those who need to know, and only for the good of the person and the protection of others.
