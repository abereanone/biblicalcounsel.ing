Because something in your heart wants it more than it wants God in that moment. Sinful patterns are driven by desires that have grown too large; change comes as Christ becomes more precious than what those desires promise.

## Proofs
- James 1:14-15
- James 4:1-3
- Romans 7:18-25
- Galatians 5:16-17

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

James explains how sin works: "each one is tempted when by his own evil desires he is lured away and enticed. Then after desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin" (James 1:14-15). Fights and quarrels come "from the passions at war within you" (James 4:1).

Notice that the problem is not only the action. It is the wanting underneath it. A man who explodes in anger at traffic may want control, or time, or respect. A woman who cannot stop shopping may want comfort or significance. The desire may not be evil in itself, but when it rules the heart, it leads to sin when it is denied.

Paul knew the struggle: "I have the desire to do what is good, but I cannot carry it out" (Romans 7:18). His answer was not a new technique but a person: "Thanks be to God, through Jesus Christ our Lord!" (Romans 7:25).

Practical questions to ask when you fall into the same sin again:

1. What did I want just before I sinned?
2. What did I fear losing?
3. What was I trusting to give me what I wanted?
4. What does Christ offer that this desire promised but could not give?
