No. God made us body and soul, and the body's weaknesses and illnesses affect how we think and feel. Biblical counselors encourage good medical care, sleep, exercise and nourishment, while recognizing that the body cannot be blamed for the heart's choices.

## Proofs
- Psalm 139:14
- 2 Corinthians 4:16
- 1 Timothy 4:8
- 1 Timothy 5:23
- 1 Kings 19:4-8

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Scripture treats us as embodied souls. "I am fearfully and wonderfully made" (Psalm 139:14). Paul told Timothy to take a little wine "because of your stomach and your frequent ailments" (1 Timothy 5:23), and he acknowledged that physical exercise has value (1 Timothy 4:8).

When Elijah collapsed in despair under the broom tree, wanting to die, God's first response was not a lecture. He let him sleep and sent an angel with food, twice (1 Kings 19:4-8). Only then did He speak to him.

So a good biblical counselor will ask about sleep, diet, illness, medication, hormones and injuries, and will urge a medical evaluation when symptoms suggest a physical cause. Thyroid disease, sleep apnea, head injuries and many other conditions can look like emotional or spiritual problems.

But the body is not the whole story. "Though our outer self is wasting away, yet our inner self is being renewed day by day" (2 Corinthians 4:16). A weak body can make obedience harder; it does not make sin necessary. Biblical counseling cares for both: the body with wisdom, the heart with the Word.
