Discipline is training a child's heart toward God through instruction, correction and example. It should be consistent, calm and loving, aimed at the child's heart and not merely his behavior, and never done in anger.

## Proofs
- Ephesians 6:4
- Deuteronomy 6:6-7
- Hebrews 12:10-11
- Colossians 3:21
- Proverbs 22:6

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

"Fathers, do not provoke your children to wrath; instead, bring them up in the discipline and instruction of the Lord" (Ephesians 6:4). Discipline is more than punishment. It includes teaching, training, correcting and modeling.

Principles:

- **Teach constantly.** God's words are to be taught "when you sit at home and when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up" (Deuteronomy 6:7). Most discipline happens in ordinary conversation.
- **Aim at the heart.** Ask what your child wanted and why, not only what he did. Point him to his need for Christ, not only better behavior.
- **Be consistent.** Clear expectations and predictable consequences help children feel secure.
- **Discipline in love, never in anger.** God disciplines us "for our good" (Hebrews 12:10). If you are angry, wait until you are calm.
- **Do not provoke or discourage.** "Fathers, do not provoke your children, so they will not become discouraged" (Colossians 3:21). Harshness, sarcasm, favoritism and unrealistic demands crush children.
- **Model repentance.** When you sin against your children, confess it to them and ask their forgiveness.
- **Restore.** After correction, reassure them of your love.

Any discipline that injures a child is abuse, not discipline, and must be reported.
