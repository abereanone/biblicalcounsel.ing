To what is unseen and eternal: to God's character, to Christ who suffered and now reigns, and to the glory He is preparing. Paul did not deny his pain, but he did not keep his eyes fixed on it.

## Proofs
- 2 Corinthians 4:16-18
- Hebrews 12:2
- Colossians 3:1-2
- Psalm 121:1-2

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Suffering pulls our attention inward and downward: to the pain, the symptoms, the memories, the fear of what comes next. That is natural. But Paul shows another direction: "we fix our eyes not on what is seen, but on what is unseen. For what is seen is temporary, but what is unseen is eternal" (2 Corinthians 4:18).

He wrote that in the middle of real affliction. His "outer self" was "wasting away," yet his "inner self" was "being renewed day by day" (2 Corinthians 4:16). The reason was where he looked.

Practically, this means:

- **Look to Christ.** "Let us fix our eyes on Jesus... who for the joy set before Him endured the cross" (Hebrews 12:2).
- **Look up.** "I lift up my eyes to the hills. From where does my help come? My help comes from Yahweh, the Maker of heaven and earth" (Psalm 121:1-2).
- **Look ahead.** Remember the glory to come, which makes present affliction "light and momentary" by comparison (2 Corinthians 4:17).
- **Do not make your sensations the main object of attention.** Notice them, care for your body, and then turn your mind to what God says.

Matthew Statler has written on this passage as a guide for sufferers and their counselors.

> **From the *Catechism of Suffering*, question 54:** How does this future hope shape our present suffering?
>
> "It teaches us that present afflictions are light and momentary, preparing us for an eternal weight of glory."
>
> Matthew Statler, [suffering.catechize.ing](https://suffering.catechize.ing/questions/cos-54)
