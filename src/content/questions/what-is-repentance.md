Repentance is turning from sin to God: seeing your sin as God sees it, grieving over it as an offense against Him, confessing it, and turning to walk in obedience, trusting Christ for forgiveness and help.

## Proofs
- 2 Corinthians 7:10
- Acts 3:19
- Psalm 51:1-12
- Proverbs 28:13
- 1 John 1:9

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

"Repent, then, and turn back, so that your sins may be wiped away" (Acts 3:19). Repentance is a change of mind that becomes a change of direction.

Paul distinguishes two kinds of sorrow: "Godly sorrow brings repentance that leads to salvation without regret, but worldly sorrow brings death" (2 Corinthians 7:10).

- **Worldly sorrow** is sorry about consequences: getting caught, losing face, feeling bad. It focuses on self.
- **Godly sorrow** is sorry for the sin itself, because it offended God and hurt others. It turns toward God.

Psalm 51, David's prayer after his adultery and murder, shows what true repentance looks like: honest confession ("against You, You only, have I sinned"), appeal to God's mercy, desire for a clean heart, and longing to be restored.

Repentance is not penance. We do not pay for our sins by feeling bad enough. "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9). It also bears fruit: making things right where we can, and putting on new habits in place of old ones.
